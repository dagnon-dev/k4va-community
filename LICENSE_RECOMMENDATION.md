# License Recommendation

The project owner selected MPL-2.0 for the initial public release. The comparison below records the alternatives considered before that decision.

| License | Permissions | Obligations | SaaS implication | Commercial implication | Patents | Contributions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Apache-2.0 | Broad use, modification and redistribution | Preserve notices and license; mark changes | Does not require offering hosted source | Permissive and business-friendly | Express patent license | Contributor license terms are clear |
| MIT | Very broad and simple use | Preserve copyright and permission notice | No source-sharing requirement | Very permissive, minimal protection | No explicit patent grant | Simple, but less defensive |
| MPL-2.0 | File-level copyleft with commercial use | Publish modifications to MPL files; preserve notices | Generally does not require the whole SaaS to be open | Balances openness and proprietary modules | Express patent provisions | Changes to covered files remain shareable |
| AGPL-3.0 | Strong copyleft with commercial use allowed | Disclose corresponding source when conveying or providing network access | SaaS modifications generally trigger source-offer obligations | Strongest reciprocity; may reduce proprietary adoption | Strong patent terms | Contributions remain under copyleft obligations |

Decision: MPL-2.0. It provides file-level reciprocity, an express patent license and permits larger works to use different terms while keeping modifications to covered files available under MPL-2.0. This is an engineering comparison, not legal advice.
